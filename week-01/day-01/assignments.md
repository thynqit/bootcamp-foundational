# Assignments – Day 1: Cybersecurity Fundamentals

> Part of the Thynqit Labs – Foundational Engineering Bootcamp

---

## 📌 Overview

These exercises help you think about cybersecurity from the perspective of a professional working in an organization.

The goal is not to build security systems yet, but to develop **security awareness and responsible behavior** when handling company systems, devices, and data.

---

## 🧠 Conceptual Questions

Answer in writing:

1. Why are employees often considered one of the biggest cybersecurity risks in organizations?

2. What is phishing and how do attackers use it to steal credentials or sensitive information?

3. Explain what social engineering is and why it is effective.

4. What types of sensitive information must engineers protect inside an organization?

5. Why should engineers be careful when installing third-party software or tools?

Focus on understanding rather than memorizing definitions.

---

## 🛠 Practical Exercises

### Exercise 1 – Identify Phishing Signals

Search for examples of phishing emails online.

Identify at least **three phishing indicators**, such as:

- suspicious links
- urgent language
- unknown sender
- requests for credentials

Write a short explanation describing how you identified the phishing signals.

---

### Exercise 2 – Security Risk Identification

Consider the following scenario:

> A teammate asks you on Slack to send a confidential document to their personal email because they cannot access their work account.

Answer the following:

- What security risks exist in this situation?
- What should you do instead?
- How should sensitive information normally be shared?

---

### Exercise 3 – Personal Security Review

Review your own digital habits.

Answer the following questions:

- Do you reuse passwords across multiple services?
- Do you use multi-factor authentication (MFA)?
- Do you install software from unverified sources?

Write down **three improvements** you could make to improve your personal security practices.

---

## 🧩 Scenario-Based Thinking

You are working on a production system that stores user information.

Consider the following situations:

- A developer accidentally commits an API key to a Git repository.
- A colleague clicks a phishing link and enters company credentials.
- A laptop containing company data is lost.

For each scenario:

1. Explain what risks exist.
2. Describe what actions should be taken.
3. Explain how the situation could have been prevented.

---

## 📋 Self-Evaluation Checklist

- [ ] I understand what phishing and social engineering attacks are.
- [ ] I can identify suspicious communication attempts.
- [ ] I understand the importance of protecting company information.
- [ ] I know how careless actions can lead to security incidents.

---

## 🚀 Optional Challenge (Advanced)

Choose a **real-world cybersecurity incident** and briefly research it.

Examples include:

- ransomware attacks on organizations
- large data breaches
- credential theft incidents

Write a short summary explaining:

- what happened
- how the attack occurred
- what mistakes allowed the attack to succeed
- what engineers could learn from the incident
