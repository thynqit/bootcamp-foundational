# Day 1 – Cybersecurity Fundamentals

> Part of the Thynqit Labs – Foundational Engineering Bootcamp

---

## 📌 Overview

Modern software engineers are responsible not only for building systems but also for protecting sensitive information, infrastructure, and user data.

Cybersecurity awareness helps engineers recognize risks early and avoid common mistakes that lead to security incidents.

Many real-world breaches happen not because of advanced attacks, but because of simple human or engineering errors such as:

- clicking malicious links
- sharing confidential information incorrectly
- exposing credentials or secrets
- misusing company resources
- weak device security

Understanding basic cybersecurity practices helps engineers protect systems, organizations, and users.

- Understand common cybersecurity threats faced by organizations

## 🧠 Core Concepts

### 1. Recognizing Phishing Attacks

Phishing attacks attempt to trick individuals into revealing confidential information such as passwords or credit card numbers.

Attackers often use:

- urgency
- emotional manipulation
- fake incentives

Examples include messages like:

- “Your bank account has been compromised”
- “You received a gift card”
- “Verify your login immediately”

Security practices:

- Always verify links before clicking.
- Hover over links to inspect the actual destination.
- Be cautious of unexpected attachments.
- Do not enter credentials on unfamiliar websites.

On mobile devices, holding your finger over a link will preview its destination.

---

### 2. Social Engineering Threats

Social engineering attacks manipulate people into giving away sensitive information.

Attackers may:

- impersonate trusted individuals
- create urgency
- pressure employees into quick decisions

Examples:

- Someone calling and pretending to be IT support
- Requests for confidential information through unexpected channels
- Messages demanding immediate action

Best practices:

- Pause and verify requests.
- Do not share credentials or sensitive information.
- Report suspicious requests to the security team.

---

### 3. Protecting Company Information

Organizations rely on employees to safeguard sensitive information.

Examples of sensitive information include:

- internal business plans
- customer data
- confidential documents
- intellectual property

Security practices:

- Do not share confidential information with unauthorized individuals.
- Avoid discussing sensitive topics in public spaces.
- Print confidential documents only when necessary.
- Properly dispose of documents using shredders.

---

### 4. Safe Use of Company Resources

Company systems and devices should be used responsibly.

Security practices include:

- Using company email only for business communication
- Avoiding sending company data to personal email accounts
- Keeping software and devices updated
- Avoiding installation of unapproved software

Unauthorized tools and services can introduce serious security risks.

---

### 5. Managing Third-Party Applications

Third-party tools and cloud services can expose sensitive data if used improperly.

Security practices:

- Download software only from approved sources.
- Avoid uploading company data to external services.
- Seek security approval before using new tools.

---

### 6. Device Security

Work devices often contain sensitive information.

Security practices:

- Do not allow others to use company devices.
- Keep devices stored securely when not in use.
- Avoid leaving devices unattended in public spaces.
- Immediately report lost or stolen devices.

---

### 7. Data Privacy Awareness

Personal data is any information that can identify an individual.

Examples include:

- names
- birthdates
- addresses
- identification numbers
- contact information

Organizations have legal responsibilities to protect personal data.

Engineers must understand how personal data is:

- collected
- processed
- stored
- shared
- deleted

Improper handling of personal data can lead to serious legal and reputational consequences.

---

### 8. Safe Communication Habits

Communication channels should be used responsibly.

Security practices include:

- avoiding sharing confidential information through unapproved platforms
- verifying recipients before sending sensitive information
- being cautious about discussing business topics on personal communication apps

---

## 🌍 Real-World Relevance

Cybersecurity failures often occur due to simple mistakes such as:

- employees clicking phishing emails
- developers committing secrets to code repositories
- unauthorized software installations
- weak device security

Many major data breaches started with small human errors.

Security awareness helps engineers identify risks before they become incidents.

## 🧩 Practical Understanding

Imagine you are working on a production system.

Consider the following:

- How could attackers obtain developer credentials?
- What sensitive information does your system store?
- What risks exist if company data is shared improperly?
- What safeguards could reduce these risks?

Thinking through these questions helps engineers design safer systems.

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

Frequent security mistakes include:

- clicking suspicious links without verification
- sharing confidential information through untrusted channels
- installing unapproved software
- leaving devices unattended
- exposing sensitive data accidentally

Security awareness helps prevent these mistakes.
