## 📌 Overview

Agile is a mindset for building software in iterative, collaborative, and adaptive ways.

Scrum is one of the most widely used Agile frameworks.

While SDLC defines structured development phases, Agile defines how teams operate within those phases — especially in environments where requirements evolve.

This module explains:

- What Agile really means
- What Scrum is (and what it is not)
- How modern engineering teams plan and deliver work

## 🧠 Core Concepts

### 1. What is Agile?

Agile is a mindset guided by the Agile Manifesto:

- Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
- Working software over comprehensive documentation
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
- Responding to change over following a plan

Agile does not eliminate planning — it emphasizes adaptability.

---

### 2. Why Agile Emerged

Traditional models struggled when:

- Requirements changed frequently
- Feedback cycles were long
- Products needed rapid iteration
- Innovation required experimentation

Agile reduces risk by delivering small, working increments.

---

### 3. What is Scrum?

Scrum is a structured Agile framework that organizes work into short cycles called **Sprints** (typically 1–4 weeks).

It includes:

- Defined roles
- Structured ceremonies
- Transparent backlog management
- Continuous improvement

---

### 4. Scrum Roles

- **Product Owner** – Defines priorities and manages backlog
- **Scrum Master** – Facilitates process and removes blockers
- **Development Team** – Builds and delivers increments

Each role has clear responsibilities.

---

### 5. Scrum Ceremonies

- Sprint Planning
- Daily Standup
- Sprint Review
- Sprint Retrospective

These ensure transparency and alignment.

---

### 6. Sprint Lifecycle

1. Backlog refinement
2. Sprint planning
3. Development
4. Review
5. Retrospective
6. Repeat

Each sprint delivers a working increment.

---

### 7. Agile vs “No Process”

Agile is not:

- Lack of documentation
- No deadlines
- No structure
- Constant chaos

Agile requires discipline, transparency, and accountability.

---

## 🌍 Real-World Relevance

In production environments:

- Agile helps adapt to customer feedback
- Scrum ensures predictable delivery cycles
- Sprint reviews prevent large-scale failures
- Retrospectives improve team performance

Most modern product companies use some form of Agile.

---

## 🧩 Practical Understanding

Scenario:

You are building a SaaS platform.

Customer feedback suggests major UX improvements after initial release.

Under Waterfall:  
Changes are expensive and delayed.

Under Agile:  
Changes can be prioritized into upcoming sprints.

How does this reduce business risk?

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Believing Agile means no documentation
- Treating Scrum ceremonies as optional
- Skipping retrospectives
- Overloading sprints
- Confusing velocity with productivity
