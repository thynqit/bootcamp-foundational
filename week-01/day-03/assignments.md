# Assignments – Day 3: Agile & Scrum Framework

> Part of the Thynqit Labs – Foundational Engineering Bootcamp

---

## 📌 Overview

These exercises help you apply Agile principles and the Scrum framework to realistic engineering scenarios.

The goal is to understand:

- why Agile emerged
- how Scrum structures team collaboration
- how iterative delivery reduces project risk
- how engineering teams plan and manage work

---

## 🧠 Part 1 – Conceptual Understanding

Answer in your own words:

1. What problem was Agile created to solve?
2. How is Agile different from traditional project management?
3. What are the four values of the Agile Manifesto?
4. What are the three primary Scrum roles and their responsibilities?
5. What is the purpose of a Sprint Retrospective?

Avoid copying definitions. Focus on clarity and reasoning.

---

## 🔍 Part 2 – Agile vs Waterfall Comparison

Create a comparison table including:

- Requirement flexibility
- Planning style
- Feedback frequency
- Risk exposure
- Documentation level
- Customer involvement

After completing the table, answer:

- Why is Agile often preferred in product companies?
- Why might regulated industries still prefer structured approaches?

---

## 🗂 Part 3 – Sprint Planning Simulation

You are part of a team building a simple **Task Management Web App**.

### Backlog Items

- User Registration
- Login & Logout
- Create Task
- Edit Task
- Delete Task
- Mark Task as Completed
- Email Notification
- Dark Mode UI

### Exercise

1. Choose **5 items** for Sprint 1.
2. Justify your selection.
3. Write a simple **Sprint Goal**.
4. Estimate effort qualitatively (**Small / Medium / Large**).
5. Identify potential risks.

---

## 🧩 Part 4 – Role-Based Thinking

For the same project:

- What would the **Product Owner** focus on?
- What would the **Scrum Master** focus on?
- What would the **Development Team** focus on?

Explain responsibilities clearly.

---

## ⚠️ Part 5 – Identify Agile Anti-Patterns

Consider the following situations:

1. The team skips retrospectives because “they are busy.”
2. Sprint scope changes daily.
3. Product Owner does not prioritize backlog.
4. Daily standups turn into 1-hour technical meetings.

For each:

- What is wrong?
- What principle is being violated?
- How should it be corrected?

---

## 🔄 Part 6 – Reflection

Answer briefly:

1. Why does Scrum enforce time-boxed sprints?
2. How does Agile reduce project risk?
3. Can Agile work without discipline?
4. Why is transparency important in Scrum?

---

## 📋 Self-Evaluation Checklist

- [ ] I understand Agile values clearly.
- [ ] I can explain Scrum roles confidently.
- [ ] I can simulate a basic sprint plan.
- [ ] I understand common Agile failures.
- [ ] I can distinguish between flexibility and chaos.

## 🚀 Optional Challenge (Advanced)

Research a real-world product company.

- How often do they release features?
- How do they collect user feedback?
- Do they use Scrum or a variation of Agile?
- What advantages does that give them?
