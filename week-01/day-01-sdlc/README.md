> Part of the Thynqit Labs – Foundational Engineering Bootcamp

## 📌 Overview

The Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC) defines the structured process used to build, test, and deliver software systems.

It provides a framework for:

- Planning work
- Managing risk
- Ensuring quality
- Delivering value incrementally

Without a defined lifecycle, software projects often suffer from scope creep, unclear ownership, poor quality, and missed deadlines.

## 🧠 Core Concepts

### 1. What is SDLC?

SDLC is a structured process for building software systems from idea to deployment and maintenance.

Typical phases include:

1. Requirement Gathering
2. Design
3. Development
4. Testing
5. Deployment
6. Maintenance

These phases may be sequential or iterative depending on the model used.

---

### 2. Waterfall Model

Waterfall is a linear, sequential model where each phase must be completed before moving to the next.

Characteristics:

- Fixed requirements upfront
- Heavy documentation
- Late feedback cycles
- High risk of requirement mismatch

Waterfall works best when requirements are stable and predictable.

---

### 3. Agile Model

Agile emphasizes:

- Iterative development
- Continuous feedback
- Incremental delivery
- Collaboration with stakeholders

Instead of building everything at once, Agile delivers software in small increments.

This reduces risk and improves adaptability.

---

### 4. Iterative vs Incremental Development

Iterative:
Improving a feature repeatedly over time.

Incremental:
Delivering small working features step by step.

Modern software teams often use both approaches.

---

### 5. Why SDLC Matters

Without SDLC:

- Deadlines become unpredictable
- Quality decreases
- Teams misalign
- Scope increases uncontrollably
- Production defects increase

SDLC introduces discipline and predictability.

---

## 🌍 Real-World Relevance

In production systems:

- Poor requirement gathering leads to rework.
- Lack of structured testing causes critical bugs.
- No deployment process leads to outages.

Most large-scale failures are process failures, not coding failures.

Understanding SDLC prevents chaos.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Believing SDLC is unnecessary in small projects
- Confusing Agile with lack of planning
- Skipping documentation entirely
- Treating testing as optional
